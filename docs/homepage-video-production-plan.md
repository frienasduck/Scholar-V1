## 2. Shared art direction

The ten supplied reference images are storyboards: warm black, bronze/amber rim lighting, ivory editorial type, translucent glass, careful depth, and cinematic framing. Use the corresponding reference for shot balance, panel relationships, lighting, and transitions—not as a single flat full-frame image with a Ken Burns effect. Keep the canonical Aurora LAM identity; the classical bust in some references is not a replacement mascot.

Use actual Scholar interface capture as the picture's main subject. Compose it in a restrained editorial stage with motivated camera moves, readable crops, occasional original diagrams, and semantic motion graphics. A camera move cannot substitute for a feature changing state. Each film must show at least three genuine activity changes, such as a choice followed by its result, a question followed by checked feedback, or a page followed by an annotation.

Suggested shared rhythm: a 1-second establishing composition, a 3–4-second input/action shot, a 3–4-second resulting state, a 3–4-second second meaningful action, and a 1–2-second settle/loop seam. Prefer 14–18 seconds at 30 fps. Hold key results long enough to read, and avoid fast cuts, strobing rims, or illegible tiny dashboards. Use a graceful editorial loop or a quiet final hold; do not reverse answer submission, task completion, or a live message to fabricate a seamless loop.

No soundtrack is required for the autoplay deliverable. If an optional user-initiated soundtrack is commissioned later, it needs documented synchronization rights and an explicit audio control. Do not reuse copyrighted tracks from the Study Music catalog.

1. Freeze the approved localhost build and list the route/session used for each take. Keep staging, production databases, credentials, personal files, and the desktop notification tray out of the capture.
2. Prefer real `/ai-tutor`, `/exam-prep`, `/resources`, `/files`, `/chapter-command`, `/practice`, `/mock-exam`, `/planner`, `/group-study`, `/ebook`, and `/focus` routes when accessible with legitimate local/test identities and materials. Do not bypass auth or Plus gates for filming.
3. Where access or a live provider is unavailable, capture the existing explicitly scripted public demonstration and retain that qualification. Do not silently restyle that demonstration as evidence of the full product.
4. Use Scholar-authored synthetic material: a one-page Laws of Motion note, a cell-biology reading, a handwritten synthetic photosynthesis page, reviewed physics questions, and a small public-domain or original reading sample. Record author/license for every imported item. NCERT or external-source listing is not permission to reuse an entire book or third-party logo in marketing.
5. Capture clean 16:9 at 1920×1080 or higher, 30 fps minimum where supported. Use deterministic cursor movement and real controls; no password fields, personal identifiers, API keys, browser permission dialogs, account emails, or secret URLs in frame. Do not load unrelated browser tabs.
6. Keep raw captures unmodified. Edit from copies; record cuts, speed ramps, retiming, substituted take boundaries, and any compression. If processing time is removed, use a visible “Sequence shortened” label where its omission would imply an unrealistic real-time result.
7. Build camera moves around readable content. Restrict perspective to mild angles; questions and annotations should face the camera when read. Use vector title/formula layers in the edit for clarity, and also supply those facts as HTML outside the encoded picture.
8. Review academic correctness against the filmed source, then check product/access claims independently. Export the sample; inspect real file metadata and several nonadjacent frames; verify actual browser playback before making the remaining nine.

## 16. Homepage playback integration and acceptance

The final design uses actual `<video>` elements as the primary section visual; useful local samples may remain under secondary “Explore the sample” disclosures. Preserve the current hero, account form, navigation, and footer. Important headings, feature descriptions, provenance qualifications, CTAs, playback status, and controls stay accessible HTML, not only burned-in pixels.

- Declare muted, loop, and `playsInline`; attempt autoplay only when allowed by user preferences and policy. Do not treat a rejected `play()` promise as successful playback.
- Before a section approaches the viewport, retain a poster and no attached video URL. Use a modest approaching-viewport margin to attach/load a source, then play only when the video is meaningfully visible.
- A shared playback coordinator selects one active showcase. Pause other feature videos and avoid leaving offscreen sources attached indefinitely. Coordinate with the existing hero so the hero and showcase do not decode concurrently when the hero is out of view. If decoder/resource release is claimed, prove source removal plus `load()` behavior rather than just calling `pause()`.
- Pause on `document.hidden`, offscreen transition, user pause, navigation/unmount, or preference change. Clean up observers, listeners, and timers. Do not auto-resume after an explicit user pause until the user requests it.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`, available reduced-data/save-data signals, and an explicit site motion preference. Default these users to a useful poster and manual “Play demonstration.” Missing network APIs are not evidence that data-saving is disabled.
- Keep status honest: “Poster · press play,” “Loading demonstration,” “Playing recorded sample,” “Paused,” “Replay,” and a recoverable “Video unavailable” state. No indefinite spinner, invisible film-sized hole, or “Playing” status before frames advance.
- Provide keyboard-operable named pause/play/replay controls with visible focus and at least 44-pixel mobile targets. Avoid announcing every timestamp in a live region. Do not make a play overlay's click also trigger unrelated section navigation.
- Preserve a stable 16:9 frame to avoid layout shifts. Mobile may stack HTML overlays below the picture; maintain readable titles at 320 pixels without clipping video controls. Prefer `object-fit: contain` when cropping would conceal a result.
- Test actual local MP4 and WebM serving, MIME types, range requests, metadata, frame progression, normal/reduced-motion/reduced-data behavior, offscreen/tab-hidden pause, only-one-playing ownership, replay from zero, unavailable-file failure, mobile layout, and tab navigation. Capture both DOM media state and decoded-frame/time progression, not just a screenshot of a poster.
- Inspect film content at start, middle, and end; verify genuine state/activity changes. The built website passes the video requirement only when actual encoded assets exist and are shown to play. A unit mock of `HTMLMediaElement.play` is not that verification.
